About Isabela

The largest province in Luzon — a tapestry of natural wonders, agricultural richness, and cultural heritage.

Province Overview

The Queen City of the North

Isabela is a province located in the Cagayan Valley region (Region II) of the Philippines. It is the largest province in Luzon and the second largest in the entire Philippines, covering approximately 10,665.48 square kilometers. The province is bounded by the Cagayan River to the east, the Sierra Madre Mountains to the east and northeast, and the Cordillera Administrative Region to the west.


Formerly part of the old Cagayan province, Isabela was established as a separate province on May 7, 1856. The name "Isabela" was given in honor of Queen Isabella II of Spain. Its capital is Ilagan City — declared as a city in 2012 — while Santiago City and Cauayan City serve as major commercial centers of the province.

Location

Located in Region II (Cagayan Valley), northeastern Luzon. Bounded by Cagayan to the north, Nueva Vizcaya to the south, Aurora and Quirino to the east.

History

Separated from Cagayan province in 1856 under Spanish colonial rule. Named after Queen Isabella II of Spain. Rich pre-colonial heritage from the Ibanag, Gaddang, and Yogad peoples.

Known Attractions

Magat Dam and Reservoir, Cagayan River, Sierra Madre forests, Disulap River System, Santa Victoria Cave, Palanan Wilderness, and Tumauini Church.

Culture & Traditions

Influenced by Ibanag, Ilocano, Gaddang, and Yogad cultures. Known for the Bambanti Festival, traditional weaving, and warm Isabeleño hospitality.
